Roofing website SEO checklist for contractors

Your website should help nearby property owners understand what you do, trust your company and request an estimate. Use this checklist to review the basics before spending more time or money on promotion.

Important: SEO is an ongoing process, not a one-time setting. These steps create a clearer foundation, but no checklist can guarantee a particular Google position or a specific number of leads.

1. Make every important service clear

  • Describe your actual services. Explain whether you handle roof replacement, repairs, inspections, storm damage or other services you genuinely offer.
  • Use a dedicated service page when useful. Give each major service enough original information to answer customer questions instead of repeating the same paragraph across many pages.
  • Write a specific page title and heading. Make the service and business purpose clear in natural language. Avoid stuffing the same keyword into every sentence.
  • Show the next step. Make estimate requests, phone contact and service details easy to find.

2. Strengthen local trust

  • Keep business details consistent. Use accurate company name, phone number, service area and hours wherever the business is listed.
  • Show genuine proof. Use real project photos with permission, explain the work performed and publish authentic customer reviews only with appropriate permission.
  • Be accurate about coverage. Mention the towns or areas actually served. Do not create misleading location pages for places the company does not serve.
  • Complete the Google Business Profile if eligible. The business owner should manage it, verify details and keep service information current.

3. Make the mobile experience simple

  • Test on a real phone. Check that text is readable, menus work and visitors do not need to zoom or scroll sideways.
  • Make contact easy. Use working tap-to-call links and a short estimate form or clear contact method.
  • Use appropriately sized images. Compress photos, use modern formats where practical and avoid loading huge images when a smaller version will look just as good.
  • Keep the page focused. Remove intrusive pop-ups and unnecessary elements that make the service or contact action hard to find.

4. Check technical SEO basics

  • Use crawlable page links. Important pages should be reachable through normal HTML links, not hidden behind a search box or script-only interaction.
  • Give each page a unique title and description. Write an accurate summary for people who may see the page in search results; Google may choose a different snippet.
  • Use a sitemap and robots.txt correctly. The sitemap should list canonical pages that you want search engines to discover. Robots.txt is not a way to force indexing.
  • Check Search Console. Review indexing reports, crawl issues, mobile usability signals where available, impressions and clicks. A successful live test does not necessarily mean a page is indexed.
  • Protect URL changes. If a page URL must change, set up an appropriate permanent redirect and update internal links and the sitemap.

5. Publish useful answers—not filler

Write content based on real questions customers ask, such as how an inspection works, what information is needed for an estimate, or how to compare repair and replacement options. Keep advice accurate, explain when an on-site assessment is needed, and review information that may change. Do not publish dozens of near-identical city pages or copied articles just to target more keywords.

For performance, use Google's PageSpeed Insights to identify the biggest real issues before making changes. A single score is a diagnostic signal, not a ranking guarantee.

6. Measure what matters

In Google Search Console, compare impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position over time. Review queries that are relevant to the services actually offered. Track quote requests and calls separately where possible, because more impressions do not automatically mean more customers.

Where to start

  1. Make sure the main service pages are useful and accessible.
  2. Check the mobile contact journey from a customer's point of view.
  3. Correct inaccurate business listings and add genuine project proof.
  4. Use Search Console data to choose the next improvement rather than guessing.
