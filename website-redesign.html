What we look at during a redesign

First impression

Does the first screen clearly explain what the business does and who it serves?

Mobile usability

Are text, navigation, buttons, and contact details comfortable to use on smaller screens?

Enquiry journey

Can a potential customer find services, understand the next step, and contact the business without friction?

A practical redesign process

  1. Review: inspect key pages, mobile layout, navigation, links, and calls to action.
  2. Prioritize: identify the most important improvements instead of changing things just for decoration.
  3. Refresh: improve page hierarchy, copy clarity, responsive behavior, and visual consistency.
  4. Quality check: review page titles, descriptions, internal links, and contact flows before launch.

Protect what already works

A redesign should not automatically discard useful content or established URLs. Where possible, retain valuable pages, map any URL changes carefully, and check redirects before launch. Search visibility can fluctuate during a redesign, so technical checks matter as much as appearance.

Website redesign FAQs

Do I need a full rebuild?

Not always. The right scope depends on the current site's technical condition, content, and the improvements needed.

Will a redesign improve SEO?

It can improve the technical and user-experience foundation, but rankings are not guaranteed. Content quality, competition, relevance, and other signals also matter.

Can existing page URLs stay the same?

Usually they can where practical. If URLs must change, create a redirect plan and update internal links and sitemap entries.
